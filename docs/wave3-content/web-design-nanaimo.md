## Proposed page copy

# Help customers check the fit before they ask for a quote

If your Nanaimo business serves more than one community, “serving Vancouver Island” may leave an important question unanswered: will you come to this customer's location for this kind of job? Your website can explain the boundary without turning every place name into another page.

EuroDigital serves Nanaimo remotely from Victoria. There is no Nanaimo office, and I have not yet built a website for a Nanaimo business. You can inspect Island work, but it should not be mistaken for a local client list.

## Start with a coverage note you would actually send a customer

Write down your normal working area and any jobs you consider farther away. If coverage varies by service, explain that distinction. Use your own operating rules; the website should not imply you cover every community simply because it appears in a regional list.

That note gives the site a practical structure: what you do, where it is available, and how someone checks an uncertain location. A service-area explanation can sit alongside the relevant service rather than being hidden behind a generic contact page.

## Decide what makes an inquiry answerable

Imagine receiving “Can you help?” with no other details. What would you ask next? For a service business, that might be the job type, location, a short description, and preferred reply method. These are planning examples, not a requirement to collect every detail from every visitor.

The written scope should identify the contact or quote path the site needs. A visitor should also be able to understand the offer before starting that path. Putting all the explanation inside a form makes it harder to judge whether the business is a fit.

## Review the journey from a phone

During review, follow a concrete scenario: open a service, find its coverage, then reach the inquiry action. Check that photos and headings help explain the work and that contact details are readable. This gives feedback a purpose beyond choosing colours. It does not predict how many inquiries the site will generate.

### Can we work together without meeting in Nanaimo?

Yes. Project discussion and review run by message and call against a written scope. If you need a designer physically based in Nanaimo, this arrangement may not suit you.

### Does the portfolio prove results for businesses here?

No. The [MaestrosServices case study](/projects/maestrosservices) is a family-business website overview from elsewhere on the Island. It demonstrates website work, not Nanaimo results or an independent endorsement.

## Send the coverage problem with your brief

Include your current URL, actual working area, main services, and the information you need to answer an inquiry.
