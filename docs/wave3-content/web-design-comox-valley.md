## Proposed page copy

# One business website for the services you offer across the valley

Does your business offer the same work in Courtenay and Comox, or do customers need different information in each place? That is a useful question to settle before drawing a sitemap. Naming both communities is straightforward. Giving each page a reason to exist takes more thought.

I'm based in Victoria and work with Comox Valley businesses remotely. EuroDigital has no office in the valley, and I have not yet built a website for a business here. The portfolio shows work elsewhere on the Island, with the relationships disclosed.

## Organize around differences a customer needs to understand

When the service and next step are the same, a clear coverage section may explain both communities. When the work differs, describe the difference: what is available, who it suits, and what information someone needs before asking about it. Those details should come from your business rather than assumptions about either community.

A separate page needs useful substance. Repeating the same offer under a different town heading adds material for you to maintain without necessarily helping a visitor choose. Start with the service explanations; then decide where coverage belongs.

## Build a content list before choosing the page count

Gather the services, photos you have permission to use, questions customers ask, and any existing pages worth keeping. Mark which items are ready and which need an owner to supply or approve them. This makes it easier to distinguish a small site from a project needing several substantive pages.

If the business serves only Courtenay, say that. If it also serves Comox or other communities, list the actual coverage. A regional identity should not quietly expand your advertised working area.

## Make remote review concrete

Message and call are the working channels. Send comments against a named page or section and explain what a customer could misunderstand. “This service is unavailable in that area” is actionable feedback; it also prevents a design review from approving inaccurate copy. The written scope records content responsibilities and review dependencies so timing can reflect the material still needed.

### Do we need a page for every place we serve?

Not automatically. Start with what changes for the reader. A coverage note may be sufficient where the offer is identical. Any additional page needs verified material of its own.
